The
method.

How a project actually runs here. Five things we hold to, whatever the brief looks like.

Four projects a year, one at a time. You work with the person doing the work — there is no account layer, no junior taking the second half, and no queue behind you.

Week one is questions: what the site has to do, who it has to convince, and which number tells us it worked. Design starts once we can answer all three in a sentence each.

Every site is written as code: semantic HTML, a real component library, your content model. No page builder, no thirty plugins, nothing you have to pay a monthly fee to keep online.

Launch is the middle of the project. We set a performance budget before the first build, check it on every deploy, and come back at thirty and ninety days with what the analytics say.

Clients in London, Paris, Melbourne and Toronto. Weekly calls in your timezone, a shared board you can open at any hour, and a written summary at the end of every week.

The
studio.

Nexa Studio is a one-desk practice with a short list of collaborators for photography, copy and motion.

I started Nexa in 2021 after a decade split between a branding agency and a product team — long enough to see how often the beautiful deck and the shipped site are two different things.

So the studio does both halves. I design the thing and I write the code that runs it, which means the type scale in the file is the type scale in production, and the animation you signed off on is the one that survives the build. Clients tend to be studios, ateliers, small labels and founders who know exactly how they want to be seen and need someone who can hold that standard through to a deploy.

Based in London, working with clients wherever the calendar allows.
